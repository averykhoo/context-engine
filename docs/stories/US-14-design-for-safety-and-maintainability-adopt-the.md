> we're not building buildings here but the principles seem relevant, how would you adopt them and where would they go?

The agent mapped the regulation onto the framework and proposed: (1) a hierarchy of controls as a principle; (2) a design review when a task is promoted to NOW, carrying the F.A.M.E. questions, above a threshold; (3) a residual-risk register that travels with the work (invariants, carried into deploys); (4) the threshold. It recommended doing 1 and 3 first and 2 as one task, and asked to record this, file the work and draft the principle text.

> Okay. Add the principles too, and the FAME acronym, since we might want to use that during a projects architectural design stage, and if something goes wrong with the process we can re-derive the intent from the principles

The excerpt's text of the F.A.M.E. principle, as attached:

> 1) Forecast maintenance – Designers should understand the impact of their designs and the expected downstream maintenance works, thereby making necessary upstream design provisions.
> 2) Access for maintenance – Designers should give due considerations for all areas requiring access for inspection and maintenance, thereby making necessary design provisions.
> 3) Minimise maintenance interventions - Designers should give adequate attention to materials performance and detailing to minimise common and critical defects.
> 4) Enable simple maintenance – Designers should consciously consider standardisation and prefabricated components to facilitate easy inspection and productive maintenance.
